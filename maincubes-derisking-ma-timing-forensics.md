# maincubes: Project De-risking and M&A Timing Forensics

**As of:** 27 September 2026
**Subject:** maincubes (maincubes one GmbH and group), majority-owned by funds advised by DTCP
**Central question:** How much of the announced organic pipeline is actually de-risked, and would M&A add value before that pipeline is delivered?
**Scope:** Independent evidence review. No future acquisition targets are proposed and no companies are ranked.

---

## 0. Method and evidence caveat (read first)

**Evidence labels:** **O** Observed (directly supported by source) · **D** Derived (arithmetic or logic from O facts) · **I** Inferred (interpretation, flagged as such) · **U** Unknown.

**Confidence:** H (primary source, wording captured, or two independent sources agree) · M (primary source via extracted summary, or strong secondary) · L (single secondary, trade listing, or summary with possible extraction error).

**Collection limitation.** The research environment's network policy blocked direct retrieval of maincubes.com, dtcp.capital, art-invest.de, aoshearman.com, hoganlovells.com, tmtfinance.com, datacenterdynamics.com, capacityglobal.com, nauen.de, entwicklungsstadt.de and jsmgroup.com. Every fact was captured through a search index that extracts content from those pages. The source register points to the underlying primary page wherever the index identified one. Search extraction can paraphrase or mis-date. One extraction error was caught and corrected during research: an index summary dated DTCP's majority acquisition to May 2026; the DTCP release, the Latham & Watkins note and the LinkedIn post ID all place it on 19 September 2023. No claim is graded H unless a primary source was identified and a second source agrees. Section 9 lists the verification reads needed before any decision relies on this report.

**The de-risking ladder used throughout.** A megawatt only counts at a gate when evidence exists for that gate. Financing is never treated as evidence of deliverability.

| Gate | Definition used |
|---|---|
| G0 Announced | Named in company or sponsor material |
| G1 Land secured | Freehold purchase completed, or long lease disclosed |
| G2 Grid contracted | Signed grid connection agreement or "committed power" statement with a named capacity |
| G3 Energisation scheduled | A dated energisation or substation milestone |
| G4 Permitted | Zoning in force and a building permit for the data hall (energy-infrastructure permits noted separately) |
| G5 Under construction | Evidence of physical works on the data hall |
| G6 Pre-leased | Signed customer contract evidenced by a named customer, a handover statement or a lender/sponsor disclosure. Marketing language excluded |
| G7 Operating | Capacity live and serving customers |

## 1. Executive factual state (15 bullets)

1. **D/M.** Identifiable **operating IT capacity is about 34 MW** across five facilities: FRA01 6 MW, FRA02 ~7.4 MW, FRA03 phase 1 8 MW, BER01 8.2 MW, AMS01 ~4.7 MW. Company and financing material describe a platform of **"approximately 400 MW"** and even "over 400 MW of IT capacity". Operating capacity is therefore **~9% of the headline**. [S1, S9, S29, S32, S37, S55, S70, S75]
2. **O/M.** FRA03 (Schwalbach, 16 MW) completed its first 8 MW module in August 2026. The second phase is to be handed over "to the customer" in summer 2027. This is the only pipeline MW with physical works and a customer handover date both evidenced. [S36, S37]
3. **O/M.** **FRA04 (Dietzenbach) has slipped.** At announcement in January 2024 it was 40 MW IT on 60 MW committed power, live in 2027. The current description is **36 MW IT, commissioning in Q1 2029**. That is roughly a two-year delay and a 10% capacity cut. Physical construction has not been evidenced. [S43, S44, S47, S48]
4. **O/H.** **mainHub Berlin (Nauen)** is the best-documented development gate by gate: land purchase completed (Oct 2024), zoning legally binding (2 Jul 2025), 200 MW grid connection agreement signed with E.DIS, building permit granted for the 110 kV substation and ~6 km cable route, groundbreaking for that energy infrastructure on 24 Mar 2026, substation completion expected June 2027. [S57, S59, S61, S62, S64]
5. **U.** For mainHub, **no data hall building permit, no data hall construction start and no named customer** were found. The first building's RFS has drifted from "end of 2027" (Oct 2024) to "12/2027" (2025) to **"Q1/2028"** on the current BER02 page. [S57, S60, S65]
6. **O/M.** CEO Oliver Menzel expects maincubes to invest **~€2.5bn at Nauen alone**, of which ~€80m is power infrastructure. This single-site figure exceeds the entire **€1.775bn committed** debt of the 2026 facility. [S62, S63, S1]
7. **O/H.** The January 2026 financing is **€2.475bn: €1.775bn committed plus a €700m uncommitted accordion**, from **eleven banks and one institutional fund**. **MUFG** was Mandated Lead Arranger and the largest single lender with **over €500m** (**D:** >28% of committed). UniCredit and ING are confirmed participants. [S1, S2, S3, S7]
8. **O/M.** Investec describes the 2026 deal as an **amendment and restatement of the existing senior facility**, upsized to €2.5bn. It refinances the portfolio and funds FRA04 and mainHub. The average margin was reported at **~270 bps**. Tenor, covenants and draw conditions are **U**. [S5, S6]
9. **O/H.** The March 2023 facility was **€1.035bn: €685m committed plus a €350m accordion**, from eight banks and one institutional fund. Sustainability-linked KPIs were added later and all were met in H1 2025. [S11, S12, S14, S15]
10. **O/M.** Contracted public-sector demand is real and concentrated. **ITZBund** (the federal IT service provider) is the single tenant of **FRA02** and the build-to-suit anchor of **BER01**. **D:** ~15.6 MW, or **~46% of operating IT MW**, sits with one federal customer. [S32, S33, S54, S56]
11. **O/L.** In April 2026 management told DataCenter-Insider that the Dietzenbach and Berlin projects are **"already leased before construction begins"**. No customer, MW, tenor or contract date was disclosed. The FRA04 web page still markets "pre-leasing options". This claim is recorded as a management assertion. It does not count as G6 evidence. [S41, S43]
12. **O/H.** Ownership: DTCP entered in Sep 2022 (~45%, joint control with Art-Invest) and became majority shareholder on **19 Sep 2023**. **Art-Invest retains ~25%** and **founder-CEO Oliver Menzel remains a shareholder**. **D:** Menzel's stake is ~10% if unchanged since 2022, which implies DTCP at ~65%. The exact split is **U**. [S16, S17, S19, S20]
13. **O/M.** The investing vehicle is **DTCP Digital Infrastructure Vehicle II** (launched June 2021, ~€1bn second close Aug 2022, ~€1.6bn final close Apr 2024, targeting 8 to 10 equity investments of €150m to €250m). DTCP's successor fund reached **~€1bn first close in July 2026** (two-thirds of target). [S22, S23, S24]
14. **O/M.** DTCP runs a **second data-centre platform, GreenScale** (launched Nov 2024 with £1bn initial commitment; 170 MW in Northern Ireland and Ireland; a 300 MW grid site in Norway). **I:** DTCP's non-German data-centre growth is housed outside maincubes. [S25, S26]
15. **O/M.** International footprint is unchanged for a decade: one Amsterdam facility (AMS01, ~4.7 MW) bought from Interoute in **April 2016**. "Leading European data center operator" language recurs in every release since 2022. No new country, site or land bank outside Germany has been announced. maincubes' only disclosed M&A is that 2016 single-asset purchase. [S16, S19, S68]

**I (draw conditions).** Platform construction facilities for European colocation developers usually tie capex draws to signed leases or pre-let thresholds, loan-to-cost tests and completion guarantees. No maincubes-specific term was found. One trade commentary says lenders now "underwrite contracted megawatts" [S10]. If that holds here, mainHub capex draws depend on the same customer contracts that are not yet evidenced. The financing then confirms lender appetite for the platform. It does not confirm that mainHub or FRA04 are deliverable on the stated dates. **U** until the facility agreement terms are seen.

**Which projects are funded (O/H):** FRA04, mainHub, operating-site expansion and refinancing [S1]. FRA05 is not named in any financing release (**O**).

### 7.3 Where M&A would plausibly destroy value (I)

1. **Management bandwidth, 2026 to 2028.** Three concurrent builds, a €2.5bn campus without a named anchor and a delayed FRA04 absorb the same leadership that would run an integration.
2. **Balance-sheet competition.** Committed debt does not cover the current development programme. Acquisition equity would compete with mainHub and FRA04 equity.
3. **Lender consent.** An amended platform facility typically restricts acquisitions and additional debt. The terms are **U**. Acquiring outside the lender group's security package would need consent or separate financing.
4. **Buying what is already owned.** Power in Brandenburg, public-sector trust and build capability are all in hand.

### 7.4 Net assessment (I)

The evidence supports three distinct M&A timing windows.

| Window | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Now to first mainHub lease** | M&A is value-destructive unless it brings operating, leased capacity or Frankfurt power that the pipeline cannot supply. The dominant value lever is signing an anchor at mainHub and starting FRA04 |
| **After a mainHub anchor is signed and FRA04 is in construction (I: 2027)** | Capital and lender capacity firm up. Management bandwidth remains stretched. Bolt-on operating assets become more digestible |
| **Pre-exit (I: 2028 to 2030)** | Sponsor interest shifts toward scale and geographic breadth for exit multiple. Any "European" expansion is more likely to happen through a DTCP-level combination or a new owner than through maincubes' own balance sheet |

## 8. Independent challenge

**Hypothesis under test:** "maincubes has enough capital and a sufficiently large organic pipeline that near-term M&A could destroy value by distracting management unless an acquisition delivers a scarce market-entry position, secured power, customers or capability unavailable through the existing pipeline."

The hypothesis has three premises (capital sufficiency, pipeline size, distraction risk) and one conclusion (M&A only for scarce assets). Each was tested separately.

### 8.1 Premise 1: "enough capital". Partly falsified

- **O/D.** Committed debt of €1.775bn includes refinancing. Nauen alone is guided at ~€2.5bn. FRA04 adds an estimated ~€0.5bn (I).
- **O.** €700m of the headline €2.475bn is uncommitted.
- **U.** Draw conditions are not public. If draws are lease-linked (I), capital availability is conditional on the same customers that are not evidenced.
- **Conclusion.** Capital is sufficient for FRA03 phase 2, FRA04 and early mainHub works. It is not demonstrated for the full mainHub build. The premise holds only for the near-term programme.

### 8.2 Premise 2: "sufficiently large organic pipeline". Holds on size, fails on de-risking

- **D.** ~340 identifiable IT MW; ~250 MW grid-contracted. On size the premise holds.
- **D.** Only ~34 MW operates, ~8 MW is in data-hall construction and ~24 MW is evidenced as contracted.
- **O.** FRA04 slipped two years. mainHub RFS drifted a quarter or more. FRA05 has no evidenced land or grid.
- **Conclusion.** The pipeline is large and **power-secured**. It is **not customer-secured**. The "400 MW platform" headline overstates de-risking by an order of magnitude when measured at the construction or contract gate.

### 8.3 Premise 3: "M&A would distract management". Supported

- **O.** 2026 to 2028 carries simultaneous commissioning (FRA03 ph2), a delayed large conversion (FRA04) and a greenfield campus (mainHub).
- **O.** The organisation is young at this scale: headcount more than doubled in 2024; COO appointed Apr 2024.
- **Conclusion.** Supported. No contrary evidence found.

### 8.4 Cases where M&A would obviously accelerate value creation

| Case | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Operating, leased capacity before 2028 | Organic adds ~8 MW before 2028 | **Counterexample to the hypothesis's "near-term" framing.** An acquisition of leased operating capacity would add EBITDA the pipeline cannot, in the sponsor's likely exit window (I) |
| Frankfurt power | Rhine-Main grid effectively closed to large new connections until the mid-2030s per the DSO; FRA05 unevidenced | **Fits the hypothesis's own exception** (secured power). This is the scarcest asset class relevant to maincubes |
| European market entry | Ten years with no organic step outside DE/NL; "European" mandate repeated | Fits the exception (market entry). DTCP's GreenScale may already serve this role at sponsor level (I) |
| Hyperscale anchor relationships | None named | Weak case. Customer relationships rarely transfer with an acquired asset unless they come with the capacity (I) |

### 8.5 Does the sponsor mandate imply acquisition urgency?

- **For urgency (I):** DIV II vintage 2021/22 points to a 2027 to 2030 exit window. At today's evidence, exit buyers would underwrite ~34 MW operating and a large unleased pipeline. DTCP's July 2026 first close shows fresh capital that could fund a combination.
- **Against urgency (O/I):** DTCP runs a separate platform (GreenScale) for Ireland and the Nordics, so geographic breadth need not come through maincubes. The 2026 financing is explicitly for organic projects. No DTCP or maincubes statement mentions acquisitions. The CFO's M&A background is the only indirect signal.
- **Net (I):** Moderate urgency, concentrated in 2028 to 2030. The highest-return near-term action for exit value is converting mainHub and FRA04 from grid-contracted to leased. That is organic.

### 8.6 Verdict

**The hypothesis survives in modified form (I).** Two corrections apply.

1. Replace "enough capital" with "enough committed capital for the near-term programme; the full mainHub build needs further equity, accordion take-up or lease-conditioned draws".
2. Replace "sufficiently large organic pipeline" with "a large, power-secured and customer-unsecured pipeline".

With those corrections, the conclusion strengthens. M&A adds value only where it brings **operating leased MW before 2028** or **Frankfurt power**, which the pipeline cannot supply. Anything else competes with mainHub for management time, equity and lender consent at the point of maximum execution risk.
