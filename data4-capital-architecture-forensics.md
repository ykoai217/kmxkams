# Data4 StableCo / GrowthCo / Capital Architecture Forensics

**As of:** 27 September 2026
**Subject:** Data4 Group (Paris), controlled by Brookfield Infrastructure Fund V since 1 August 2023
**Scope:** Ownership, financing, StableCo / development-capital architecture, campus and power map, and what these imply for M&A necessity. No future acquisition targets are proposed and no companies are ranked.

---

## 0. Method and evidence caveat (read first)

**Evidence labels:** **O** Observed (directly supported by a source) · **D** Derived (arithmetic or logic from O facts) · **I** Inferred (interpretation, flagged as such) · **U** Unknown.

**Confidence:** H (primary source, wording captured) · M (primary source via extracted summary, or two agreeing secondaries) · L (single secondary, a data aggregator, or a summary with possible extraction error).

**Collection limitation.** The research environment's network policy blocked direct retrieval of data4group.com, bip.brookfield.com, sec.gov, interogoholding.com, arjuninfrastructure.com, linklaters.com, cliffordchance.com, datacenterdynamics.com and most press sites. Every fact was captured through a search index that extracts content from those pages. The source register points to the underlying document wherever the index identified one. Search extraction can paraphrase, round or mis-attribute. For that reason no claim is graded **H** unless the same figure appears in two independent extractions of a primary document. Section 10 lists the primary reads needed before anyone relies on this for a decision.

**FX convention.** Brookfield reports in US$. Data4 and lenders report in €. Where I convert, I use EUR/USD 1.10 to 1.17 (the range across April 2023 to September 2025) and state the rate used.

**Naming convention.** "Data4 GrowthCo" means the Data4 group excluding StableCo: the development platform and every asset outside the 244 MW stabilised perimeter. The term "GrowthCo" is Data4's and its lenders' own. [S16, S17, S18]

## 1. Executive factual state (15 bullets)

1. **O/H.** Brookfield Infrastructure, with institutional partners (the "Data4 consortium"), closed the acquisition of Data4 from AXA IM Alts on **1 Aug 2023**. Consideration was **US$0.6bn for Brookfield Infrastructure (BIP) and US$3.0bn for the consortium**. BIP's **effective interest is 19%**. [S6, S7]
2. **O/M.** BIP controls and consolidates Data4 through a **voting agreement with a Brookfield affiliate** that gives BIP the right to direct Data4's relevant activities. The rest of the equity sits with Brookfield Infrastructure Fund V (BIF V) limited partners and co-investors, who are not named publicly. [S7, S69]
3. **O/M.** Press reports before signing put the 2023 valuation at **~€3.5bn**. At signing Brookfield disclosed **US$2.4bn of total Brookfield equity (BIP ~US$600m)**. No public source identifies a separate holdco acquisition loan. **D:** Equity of ~€2.2bn against a ~€3.5bn EV implies ~€1.3bn of existing debt, which matches the **€1.2bn refinancing tranche** in Data4's October 2023 facility. [S4, S5, S9]
4. **O/M.** **October 2023:** Data4 raised **€2.2bn, extendable to €3.2bn**, from **BNP Paribas, Natixis, ABN AMRO and Deutsche Bank** (AXA IM also reported as involved). It comprised a **€1.2bn refinancing tranche**, a **€1.0bn tranche for new sites and construction**, and a **€1.0bn accordion option**. [S9, S10, S11]
5. **O/M.** **January 2025 (announced; worked on through H2 2024):** a **€3.3bn** package split into **€2.2bn of new term debt for "StableCo"** (Paris, Madrid and Milan hyperscale assets; "over 10 operational assets") and a **€1.1bn incremental capex facility for "GrowthCo"** ("to support further greenfield projects and acquisitions"). Reported lenders are **MUFG, Natixis, SMBC, Société Générale and ABN AMRO**, with SMBC, SocGen and Natixis named as leads. Maturities, pricing, covenants and hedging are **U**. [S16, S17, S18, S19, S21]
6. **U / L.** **MUFG's role in the 2023 acquisition financing is not publicly documented.** The only trace is one LinkedIn profile of a former MUFG banker citing "the underwriting deal team working with Brookfield on their acquisition of DATA4". MUFG's lender role is documented only for the **2025 €3.3bn package**. [S71, S16]
7. **O/H.** **StableCo sale (signed Q1 to Q2 2025; financial close announced 1 Aug 2025):** 244 MW of contracted, operating hyperscale capacity in Paris, Milan and Madrid. **Arjun Infrastructure Partners bought 30%** (O/H). **Interogo Holding** (through Inter Infrastructure Capital II) bought a "significant minority" stake. Interogo's exact percentage is **not published**. [S31, S33, S34, S38, S39]
8. **O/M.** On **4 Sep 2025**, Bloomberg reported that **Brookfield Super-Core Infrastructure Partners (BSIP) and an affiliate bought 40.1% of StableCo from Data4**, on the same terms as the Arjun and Interogo sales, with approval from both funds' advisory committees. **Data4 retains 10%** and keeps operating the assets. [S42, S43]
9. **D/M.** **Final StableCo cap table:** Arjun 30.0% · BSIP and affiliate 40.1% · Data4 (GrowthCo, BIF V-controlled) 10.0% · **Interogo 19.9% (derived as the residual)**. **Brookfield-managed vehicles hold 50.1% in total.** BIP's own filings confirm a **90% sell-down with 10% retained as an associate**, which is consistent with this table. [S28, S42, D]
10. **O/M.** BIP disclosed the **first 30% sale at ~US$460m (~US$90m net to BIP)**. It then agreed the **"incremental 60%" at US$2.4bn**, bringing total proceeds at BIP's share to **~US$300m**. The Q3 2025 6-K shows **net proceeds of ~US$310m and a gain of ~US$25m**. BAM described the transaction as a sale of stabilised Data4 assets "for **$3.6 billion**". [S26, S27, S28, S30]
11. **D/M.** **Valuation reconciliation.** 30% for US$460m gives **100% equity of ~US$1.53bn**. The US$2.4bn for 60% and the US$3.6bn for 90% both imply a **100% EV of ~US$4.0bn** (≈€3.5bn to €3.7bn; Bloomberg reported €3.7bn). EV minus equity is **~US$2.5bn, which equals the €2.2bn StableCo term debt** at ~1.12. **EV/MW ≈ €14.6m to €15.2m; LTV ≈ 60% to 62%.** [D from S26, S27, S30, S24, S16]
12. **O/M.** At acquisition, Data4 had **31 data centres in six countries, 850 MW of secured power and ~100 MW of revenue-generating capacity**. The plan was to add **400 MW**. By August 2023, **a 130 MW hyperscale MOU had been converted to a firm contract**, and more than 50% of the 400 MW plan was contracted or reserved. [S1, S5, S6]
13. **O/M.** Data4 now describes **ten campuses in six countries** and **>€20bn of European investment by 2030**. Named pipeline sites include **Paris 375 MW secured** (PAR1, PAR2, PAR3), **Escaudain 700 MW** (launched 12 Jun 2026; target 2030), **Cambrai / E-Valley 1 GW** (300 MW by 2030; ~10 years to full build), **Milan ~300 MW**, **Madrid ~150 MW**, **Hanau 180 MW**, **Athens 90 MW** and **Warsaw 60 MW**. **D:** Gross disclosed site potential is **~2.9 GW, about 12x the StableCo perimeter**. [S45, S46, S48, S52, S56, S58, S60, S61, S62]
14. **O/M.** Every Data4 market entry since 2018 was greenfield or a land/brownfield purchase: Poland (2023), Germany (Hanau, ex-military land), Greece (Paiania) and Nord France (tendered brownfields). **No completed Data4 corporate acquisition was found for 2017 to 2026 (U / negative evidence).** Financing documents from 2017, 2021 and 2025 **do reserve capacity for "acquisitions" or "external growth"**. [S14, S66, S16, S20]
15. **I.** Capital architecture is a **build, contract, stabilise, recycle** loop. GrowthCo keeps land, power and development rights. Stabilised MW are sold at ~€15m/MW into StableCo, while Data4's own disclosed project budgets run **€3.3m to €11.1m per MW (median ~€7m)**. On that evidence an acquisition of generic operating capacity would reverse the spread Data4 monetises. This **supports** the stated hypothesis. Section 9 sets out where the hypothesis weakens.

**Derivation of Interogo's stake (D).** BIP's Q3 2025 6-K records a **90% sale** with **10% retained**. Arjun is 30% (confirmed by Arjun) and BSIP is 40.1% (Bloomberg). 90.0 − 30.0 − 40.1 = **19.9%**. The earlier ION plan (Feb 2025: Arjun 30%, "Brookfield retains 30%", Data4 10%) shows the structure moved between signing and close. One search extraction said Arjun and Interogo "each acquired 30%". That conflicts with the 90% / 40.1% arithmetic, so I treat it as an extraction error. [S25, S28, S42]

**The 19.9% figure is a derived value.** A figure just below 20% is a common threshold for staying below significant-influence and certain regulatory triggers (**I**). Neither Interogo nor Data4 publishes the number.

## 6. Campus / power map (Workstream 5)

**Headline figures.** Data4 states **ten campuses in six countries** (June 2026). Aggregate figures over time: 204 MW and 129 ha across 21 DCs (2021); 843 MW of electrical reserves and ~185 ha (c.2021); 850 MW secured power and 31 DCs (2023); 35 DCs and 1 GW combined capacity (Nov 2024). ## 9. Independent challenge to the stated hypothesis

> **Hypothesis:** "Given Data4's disclosed land/power resources and capital architecture, a future acquisition would need to solve market entry, power/permitting scarcity, contracted-customer access or some other non-capital constraint; generic capacity alone is unlikely to be enough."

### 9.1 Evidence supporting it

1. **Spread arithmetic (D).** Data4 sells stabilised MW at ~€15m. Its own disclosed builds run ~€7m per MW (median). Buying generic operating capacity at market prices would pay the full stabilised value that Data4 already captures by building. This is the strongest single argument.
2. **Pipeline scale (D).** ~2.9 GW of gross disclosed site potential against 244 MW stabilised. A typical operating bolt-on (tens of MW) would add a low-single-digit percentage to the book.
3. **Capital is not scarce (O/D).** A dedicated GrowthCo capex line, ~US$1.4bn of recycled equity and Brookfield's France commitment show capital is abundant relative to deployable projects.
4. **Revealed preference (O).** Four market entries (PL, DE, GR, Nord FR) were all organic. The financing "external growth" language from 2017 and 2021 was never executed as corporate M&A.
5. **Architecture fit (I).** The StableCo / GrowthCo split exists to sell stabilised assets. An acquired operating portfolio would belong in the StableCo box, where Data4 is a 10% seller. GrowthCo holds land, power and projects.

### 9.2 Attempts to falsify it

| # | Falsification test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Explicit "acquisitions" purpose in the Jan 2025 GrowthCo facility.** If generic capacity had no value, why earmark debt for acquisitions? | **Partly survives.** The Oct 2023 facility's equivalent wording was "acquisition of new sites", i.e., land. Clifford Chance's wording is generic. Ambiguous; it does not prove an appetite for operating capacity |
| F2 | **Time-to-power.** For 2026 to 2028, energised capacity may be scarcer than Data4's pipeline suggests (Nord sites 2030+, Hanau to 2032). Buying energised, near-ready capacity could be worth more than generic MW | **Weakens the "generic capacity" limb.** An acquisition of energised capacity in an existing metro would be a *time* purchase. The hypothesis absorbs this under "power/permitting scarcity", which exposes the problem in F5 |
| F3 | **Brookfield group level.** BSIP just bought 40.1% of generic stabilised capacity at full value. So the Brookfield system *does* value generic operating capacity, as super-core product | **Falsifies the hypothesis at group level.** It holds only for Data4 GrowthCo as a platform. At Brookfield level, stabilised generic MW are a desired asset for core capital |
| F4 | **Future StableCo feedstock.** If StableCo becomes a repeat vehicle, buying stabilised third-party assets could scale it faster than Data4 can build | **Not supported by evidence.** No drop-down, ROFO or StableCo-2 disclosed. Remains a theoretical counterexample (I) |
| F5 | **Falsifiability of the wording.** "Or some other non-capital constraint" admits almost any rationale (time, talent, a tax asset, a customer contract, an operating team) | **Methodological weakness.** As phrased, the hypothesis is close to unfalsifiable. A sharper version: *"Data4 will not acquire operating capacity in a metro where it already holds unconstrained powered land, unless the asset delivers energised MW earlier than its own pipeline."* That version is testable against future deals |
| F6 | **Talent / delivery.** Hiring >100 people from outside the sector signals execution capacity as a constraint | **Consistent with the hypothesis** ("other non-capital constraint"). It adds a constraint that the hypothesis's named list omits |
| F7 | **Market gaps.** Absence from London, Amsterdam, Dublin and the Nordics despite 2021 intentions (UK, Scandinavia) | **Consistent with the hypothesis.** These are grid-constrained or land-constrained metros where entry would plausibly need an acquisition |

### 9.3 Verdict

* **Data4 GrowthCo level: hypothesis survives (M confidence).** The spread arithmetic, pipeline scale and revealed organic preference make generic capacity a poor use of Data4's capital.
* **Brookfield group level: hypothesis fails in part (M confidence).** BSIP's purchase shows that Brookfield-managed super-core capital values generic stabilised MW for its own sake.
* **Largest open risk: time-to-power (I).** If AI demand in 2026 to 2028 outruns Data4's energisation schedule, energised capacity becomes a scarce input. The hypothesis covers that only through its catch-all clause.
* **Recommended reformulation:** replace "some other non-capital constraint" with a closed list (market entry, energisation date, permit, customer contract, delivery team). Then test each future Brookfield / Data4 transaction against that list.
